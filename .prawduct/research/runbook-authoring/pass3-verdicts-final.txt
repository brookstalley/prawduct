CONFIRMED 3-0 ms-dual-compat
  Microsoft researchers building StepFly (arXiv 2510.10074; Proc. ACM Softw. Eng., Vol. 3, No. FSE, Article FSE136, 2026) declined to convert troubleshooting guides into a domain-specific language designed purely for automated execution, instead imposing a "dual-compatibility" requirement on their revision process: TSGs must remain "accessible and comprehensible to human SREs, not exclusively optimized for automated agents," because a DSL "would create significant barriers for human practitioners and complicate long-term maintenance." Their empirical study of 92 real-world TSGs from 9 teams also concluded (Finding 4) that "Most TSGs, in their current form, are not readily suitable for automation due to various quality issues" and "require substantial preprocessing or refinement before automation can be effectively implemented." Note this dual-compatibility choice is reported engineering rationale, not an experimentally evaluated comparison against a DSL alternative.

CONFIRMED 3-0 ms-onthefly
  StepFly (Mao et al., arXiv 2510.10074; Proc. ACM Softw. Eng. 3, FSE, Article FSE136, July 2026) reports that when an LLM agent must synthesize log queries on the fly from templates supplied in context — rather than invoking a pre-extracted template — this is "a primary source of error," which they characterize by three failure modes: instruction drift (the LLM ignores the template and rewrites the query), structural omissions (missing sub-queries or conditions), and syntax errors (notably incorrect regex formatting from escape-character mishandling). The paper does not quantify the relative frequency of the three. Their mitigation is to extract exact query templates offline into Query Preparation Plugins that compose the query from supplied parameters, removing on-the-fly generation from the execution path; measured gain over the same system without it is 2.5% success rate on GPT-4.1 and up to 10.63% on the weaker GPT-4.1-mini, with the benefit larger for less capable models. Evidence is from KQL log queries in incident-troubleshooting automation; extension to operational commands generally is a plausible but untested generalization.

CONFIRMED 3-0 nissist-ttm
  A Microsoft/Peking University preprint (Nissist, arXiv:2402.17531v2, 10 May 2024, Section 1) reports that in an analysis of around 1000 high-severity incidents over the recent twelve months requiring on-call engineer intervention, incidents paired with a troubleshooting guide (TSG) exhibited a 60% shorter average time-to-mitigate than those without. Treat this as weak evidence: it is a single motivating sentence in the introduction of a self-described work-in-progress paper, with no disclosed methodology, dataset, matching, or significance testing. It is observational and correlational, not experimental, and plausibly confounded — incidents with a pre-written guide are likely the better-understood, more routine ones.

CONFIRMED 2-1 itbench-ceiling
  On the ITBench-AA SRE incident-diagnosis benchmark (IBM Research + Artificial Analysis, May 2026), no frontier model reaches 50% accuracy — the top score is 47% (Claude Opus 4.7), making it one of the least saturated agentic benchmarks in the suite. KILLED 0-3 nureg-atomicity
  CORRECTED: NUREG-0899 (NRC, August 1982), Appendix B, "Conditional Statements and Logic Sequences," advises that the logic word THEN "should not be used at the end of an action to instruct the operator to perform another action within the same step, because it runs actions together," and names three reasons such embedded actions are problematic: they (1) may be overlooked and not performed, (2) make it difficult to verify performance of each action step when a check-off or sign-off is used, and (3) can be confused with a logic statement. This is guidance stated as "should," not a prohibition, and it applies only to a trailing THEN after an action — the same section endorses THEN as the consequent marker in IF / IF NOT / WHEN conditional steps.

KILLED 0-3 nureg-branching
  CORRECTED: NUREG-0899 (NRC, Aug. 1982), Appendix B, presents an acceptable method for conditional logic in emergency operating procedures: a condition-contingent step "should begin with the words IF, IF NOT, or WHEN" followed by the condition (the antecedent) and "the word THEN, followed by the action to be taken (the consequent)"; "the word AND should not be used to join more than four conditions," and beyond four "a list format should be used"; and mixing AND and OR with IF/THEN in the same step "should be avoided" because such statements "can be confusing and ambiguous" — though the guide explicitly allows it where unavoidable, provided the more explicit expanded form is used. KILLED 0-3 nureg-validation
