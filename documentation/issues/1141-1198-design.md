## Decisions

### D1 — The plan-order rule, in the one predicate that already rations path credit

**Rule.** When `chunk_proposal` names chunks for a review, a chunk proposed by a path alone is
named only if no earlier open chunk is left out of the proposal. Walk the plan's open chunks in
plan order:

- A chunk named by **words** is kept: in a whole-plan mode, a commit subject in the interval that
  carries the plan's scope and the chunk.
- A chunk credited only by a **path** is kept while every open chunk before it has been kept.
- Once one open chunk is left out, every later path-only chunk is withheld.

A `dropped` chunk and a `done` chunk are not open, so neither opens a gap. Synthetic entries are
not chunks (`current_chunks` already leaves them out).

**Where it lives.** It goes in `_deliverable_chunks`' `certifying=True` branch, which becomes the
one home of both certifying path rules: the one-owner rule and plan order. The function gains a
keyword `kept_by_words: set[str]` (normalized ids). `chunk_proposal` therefore runs its subject
walk first and passes the subject-named set in. `chunk` mode passes the empty set, as it walks
nothing today. `_PathAnswer` gains `withheld: dict[str, tuple[str, str]]`, keyed by normalized
id, with the path that proposed the chunk and the earlier open chunk left out. `certifying=False`
(`built_report`) is untouched. No second copy of the rule exists anywhere: the chunk-mode
dispatch check (D3), the Stop-path deferral (D4) and the flag (D6) all read its answer.

**What it says.** `ChunkProposal` gains `withheld: tuple[str, ...]` (ids as the plan prints them,
plan order). Each withheld chunk adds one note, in every mode including `chunk`:

> chunk 06 not proposed: this diff touches `docs/guide.md`, which only 06 lists, but chunk 05 is
> earlier in the plan, still open and not in this review, so the touch cannot say it was 06's
> work. A scoped `(Chunk 06)` commit subject or `--chunks 06` names it.

This replaces 1198-R1's cumulative-final-only rule with the general one (ruling 2), and it closes
1141's route A wherever a gap exists. Only the proposal changes. A fact is read exactly as before
(1198-NF3, 1141-NF3).

### D2 — What ruling 2 cannot mean, and the narrowest accommodation

Ruling 2 is glossed as "no chunk is certified while an earlier chunk is still open". Taken
literally, that is a rule in `chunk_states`, where facts are read: a fact would stop certifying a
chunk while an earlier one is open. That reading breaks three things, each with evidence above.

1. **It overrules an owner ruling.** `data-model.md` D1, and the #1199 departure the owner
   confirmed on 2026-11-18, certify a chunk whose scoped `(Chunk N)` commit a clean edge review
   spans, including a delegate's merged-in side, in whatever order the delegates land. A
   read-side plan-order rule would void that span until every earlier chunk is done. This design
   does not reopen D1.
2. **It breaks a sanctioned plan shape.** Delegated partitions build out of order by design. On
   the backlog-roi plan, chunk 11 was really built and reviewed, and the open chunks before it
   are uncredited work, not missing work. A read-side rule would add a ninth open chunk to a
   plan that already has eight.
3. **It reinterprets history.** Both requirements make "facts written before the change read as
   before" a constraint (1198-NF3; 1141 non-goal "Re-certifying history").

So the design applies ruling 2 where 1198-R2 (b) states it, "any chunk **proposed by path alone**
while an earlier chunk is open and unproposed", in every mode. The accommodation is the narrowest
one that keeps delegated and other out-of-order shapes working: **words still certify out of
order, and paths do not.** Words are `--chunks`, a scoped subject in a whole-plan proposal, and a
span, which is subject-only by D1. The line follows D1's own reason for taking path credit from
spans: "a path says what a commit changed, never whose work it was." A path cannot jump an open
chunk. A builder's own words can, and D5 records which words did it. D6's flag shows the state
either way.

What this leaves for the owner is in "Open questions" 1.

### D3 — `chunk` mode: a proposal of two chunks is refused (1141-R1)

The plan-order rule cannot tell adjacent chunks apart. Building 03 with 01 and 02 done, a diff
that also creates a file under a directory only 04 lists proposes `03, 04`, and both are open
with no gap (run above). A `chunk` review is one chunk's review. The rule:

**In `chunk` mode, with `--chunks` omitted, a proposal holding more than one chunk refuses the
dispatch, with exit 8 `chunks-ambiguous`.** Nothing has been written at that point: no manifest,
no marker, no archive of leftovers. The check runs just after `_proposed_chunks` in
`begin_review` and reads `ChunkProposal` (D1's answer), never a recount. The refusal is on
stdout. Per proposed chunk, it names the paths that proposed it (up to three, then "+N more").
It then gives two exact lines to re-run: the first with `--chunks <earliest>`, and the second
with `--chunks <all>` "if this review is all of their work".

- Refuse rather than narrow. Narrowing to the earliest chunk names the wrong one when the
  builder is working out of order and the diff touches an earlier chunk's path in passing.
  Refusal costs one re-run of `critic-begin` before any reviewer starts. 1141's requirements
  recommend it too.
- Exit 8 rather than 1. Skills bind to exit codes, not parsed text (`api-contract.md` Direction,
  error model), and the skill's "1 otherwise" row says "report and stop", which is the wrong
  response here. An additive sentinel fits the additive-first norm. The skill's exit table gains
  one row: **8 — chunks ambiguous (`chunk` only): re-dispatch with the `--chunks` line that matches
  the work you built; never pass both chunks to get past it**.
- Whole-plan modes do not refuse on several chunks, since naming several is their purpose. The
  plan-order rule binds them through D1.
- `verify-resolutions` is unchanged. Its proposal is the chain's named chunks less retractions,
  so it never widens, and it inherits a narrowing (1198-R3). Test (l) pins this.

### D4 — The Stop-path deferral keeps seeing work the rule withholds

The deferral's question is "does this work touch an open chunk?". The certifying proposal is a
narrower question: which chunks a review may name. Both certifying rules (one owner, plan order)
leave chunks out of the proposal that the work does touch. So `ChunkProposal` also gains
`touched: tuple[str, ...]`: every open chunk whose Deliverables cover a changed path, before
either rule, after `_work_paths`. `_deliverable_chunks` computes it in the same pass.
`critic_mode.extension_deferral_owed` (1a) changes from `if … plan_status.propose_chunks(…,
"chunk")` to `if plan_status.chunk_proposal(…, "chunk").touched:` no deferral.

Without this, two things defer the review of a chunk nobody reviewed:

- work on an open chunk that has an earlier open chunk before it, which the plan-order rule would
  newly cause;
- work on an open chunk that only touches a path it shares with another chunk, which the
  one-owner rule already causes today. This is what 1141-R3 asks to see fixed: 04's work on the
  shared module must infer a chunk review.

Both change toward reviewing more, not less. The coordinator's 2026-10-25 accepted fail-open is
untouched: a chunk with no path-shaped Deliverables, and a stub fragment, still cannot be touched.
`propose_chunks` keeps its signature; its only other readers are docstrings.

### D5 — Every `done` row says how it became done (1141-R4, R5, R6, R7)

**Recorded at dispatch** (the route of a proposal costs a git walk to recompute, so 1141-R4's
"unless it can be computed" does not hold for it):

- `chunks_declared: bool` on the manifest and the fact: whether `--chunks` was passed. Without
  it, a declared set equal to the proposal cannot be told from the proposal.
- `chunks_evidence: {<chunk id>: {"paths": [≤3], "paths_more": int, "commits": [≤3 12-char
  ids]}}`, one entry per proposed chunk, taken from `ChunkProposal` (which gains
  `evidence`, filled by `_deliverable_chunks` and the subject walk).

This is a persisted format, so its questions are listed here. It answers "was this chunk named by
the builder or by the code?" and "which path or commit made the code name it?". Its readers are
`chunk_states`' route text and the bug-report intake (D10). It is never read for state: `chunks`
stays the only thing `fact_chunks` reads (1141-NF3). The manifest validator types both keys as
optional, as it does `chunks_proposed`. `_fact_chunk_fields` copies them. `data-model.md`'s
Evidence Store entry and `review-cycle.md`'s manifest key list describe them.

**Rendered** in the `reason` of a `done` row certified by naming. The span route already uses
`reason` for this, so it becomes the one channel for "how this became done". `ChunkState`'s
docstring changes from "None for … a done one certified by name" to say that every review-done
row carries its route. No reader branches on a `done` row's `reason` being `None`. *Read, not
run:* the readers found by grep are `briefing.py` ~579, `record_lint.py` ~1083,
`plan_archive.py` ~188 and `pr_gate.py` ~380, and each reads synthetic entries only. The text is
derived from the fact alone, with no git:

| The fact records | Row text |
|---|---|
| `chunks_declared: false` | `named by its proposal: path \`lib/c04/new.py\`` / `… commit 1a2b3c4d5e6f's subject` |
| `chunks_declared: true`, chunk in `chunks_proposed` | `named by --chunks (in the proposal)` |
| `chunks_declared: true`, chunk not in `chunks_proposed` | `named by --chunks, outside the proposal (01, 02)` (1141-R6) |
| no `chunks_declared`, chunk not in `chunks_proposed` | `named by --chunks, outside the proposal (…)`: a proposal never names outside itself, so this is certain |
| no `chunks_declared`, chunk in `chunks_proposed` | `named; whether by --chunks or the proposal is not recorded (fact predates #1141)` (1141-R5) |
| bare `chunk` only, no `chunks` | `named; route not recorded (fact predates chunks_proposed)` |

All three surfaces print it through `render_table` (1141-R7): `plan status`, the `/clear`
handoff (`briefing.py` ~2346) and the PR payload. `plan status --json` already carries `reason`.

### D6 — `plan status` flags a `done` chunk with an open chunk before it (1198-R4, R5, R6)

`ChunkState` gains `ahead_of: str | None`. It is computed in one last pass of `chunk_states`
(plan order and computed states, no git, 1198-NF1). For a chunk `done` by a review fact (named or
span), it holds the earliest open non-synthetic chunk before it. Imports and box reads are not
flagged: a tick is a hand claim, and the import path is retiring. `render_table` prints one line
under the row, and `--json` rows gain an `ahead_of` key (additive).

The flag covers every out-of-order `done`, not only `Type: cumulative-final` (1198-R5 settled
toward ruling 2). Out of order is legitimate for delegated chunks, so the wording comes from the
route (D5) and never calls the state wrong:

- by a proposal, or a route that is not recorded:
  `done ahead of open chunk 05 — if chunk 06's work is not built, \`prawduct-hook plan retract
  06 --all --reason …\``
- by `--chunks` or by span:
  `done ahead of open chunk 05 (named by --chunks) — expected when chunks are built out of order;
  if 06's work is not built, \`prawduct-hook plan retract 06 --all --reason …\``

It is advice. No gate reads `ahead_of`, and it changes no state. On the backlog-roi plan it would
print under 11 in the first form, because that fact predates `chunks_declared`.

### D7 — `plan retract <chunk> --all` (1198-R7, R8, R9)

`plan_status.retraction_plan(plan_path, facts, chunk, *, repo_dir) -> list[RetractionTarget] |
RetractionTarget` is the answer, exported in place of a walker. It runs as follows:

1. Run `retraction_target(…, fact_id=None)` once for its refusals. A ticked box refuses first
   (1198-R8); `already` with nothing certifying returns `already`.
2. Collect every `review` and `chunk-imported` fact of the plan's scope that names the chunk
   (`fact_chunks`) and is not yet withdrawn for it. Each one must answer `ok` from
   `retraction_target(…, fact_id=F)`. A blocked fact that names the chunk can certify once its
   blocker resolves, so it is taken too.
3. Then loop. Add the pairs collected so far to an in-memory copy of the store as synthetic
   `chunk-retracted` envelopes, re-run `chunk_states`, and if the chunk now reads `done` by a
   span, add that fact. Stop when it is `open`, or when a refusal or a repeated fact shows up.
   The loop is bounded by the number of review facts. It only reads, so nothing is half-written
   by the planning.

The CLI writes one `chunk-retracted` fact per pair, in plan order, with the same note-on-HEAD as
today. It prints each pair and ends with the chunk's state. `--all` with `--fact` is a usage error
(exit 2). A write that fails partway prints what was written and exits 1
(`fact-append-failed`). A re-run then converges, because every pair already written answers
`already`. Without `--all`, the existing "still reads done" line adds: "or withdraw every
certification with `plan retract <chunk> --all --reason …`" (1198-R9). The cost is one
`chunk_states` per loop pass, CLI only, never on the Stop path.

### D8 — The deferral names a done chunk the work touches (1141-R8)

When `infer-critic-mode` answers `deferred` through `extension_deferral_owed`, it checks whether
the pending paths touch the Deliverables of a chunk that is `done` by a review fact. If they do,
the message ends with: "this work touches chunk 04's Deliverables, which `<fact>` certified; if
that was wrong, `prawduct-hook plan retract 04 --all --reason …`". The inference does not change,
since a later chunk editing a done chunk's file is ordinary. The check reuses
`_deliverable_chunks` over the done ids, through a new exported
`plan_status.done_chunks_touched(plan_path, states, paths) -> list[tuple[str, str]]`, so the rule
has no second copy. Tests pin the deferral message, and those assertions are renegotiated in the
open (`tests.md`). The design takes 1141's open question 3 as answered "advice in the message",
because G3 asks for it where the builder meets the state first.

### D9 — Methodology and the prose sweep (1198-R10, 1141-A4, 1198-A5)

**`planning.md` "Chunk state"** (the build writes it; proposed text):

- After the sentence defining **done**, add: "With `--chunks` omitted, the review names its
  proposal: the open chunks whose Deliverables its diff touches and, in a `final` or
  `cumulative`, those a scoped subject in its interval names. A path proposes a chunk only while
  every open chunk before it is proposed too, so a later chunk's file written early is no
  certification of it. A chunk built ahead of an open earlier one is named by its scoped
  `(Chunk N)` subject or by `--chunks`. A `chunk` review whose proposal holds two chunks is
  refused until you choose. `plan status` says how each done chunk became done, and marks one
  with an open chunk before it."
- Change the retraction sentence to: "`prawduct-hook plan retract <chunk> --reason …` reopens it
  on the record; `--all` withdraws every fact certifying it."

**`planning.md` "Plan Shape"** (ruling 1), a new bullet:

- "**A plan may ship in parts.** Each part's PR runs its own boundary `cumulative`, which
  certifies the chunks that part built and none after an open one. The plan's
  `cumulative-final` chunk stays open until the last part. Merge each part with a merge commit
  or a fast-forward: a squash merge takes part one's reviewed commits out of the history its
  certifications are read from, and they reopen. Continue on the plan's `branch:` (or update
  it). The last part's boundary `cumulative` reviews the work since the previous part merged and
  certifies what is left. When you know in advance that a plan will ship in waves, split it into
  one plan per wave instead."

**The falsifying query.** It is run over prose, tests, templates and docstrings, not limited to
the files edited: `grep -rn -e "exactly the proposal" -e "names every chunk built" -e "every open
chunk whose" -e "Omit the flag to name" plugin/ .prawduct/artifacts/ documentation/`. Each hit is
rewritten or deleted. Known hits, read:

### D10 — Bug-report intake (1141-R10, R11)

**R10.** When the component is `plan status` or chunk state, `plugin/skills/report-bug/SKILL.md`
asks for the affected row from `prawduct-hook plan status --json`. After D5 and D6, that row
carries the route and `ahead_of`. It also asks for the certifying fact's `mode`, `chunks`,
`chunks_proposed`, `chunks_declared` and `chunks_evidence`, read by `prawduct-hook evidence list`
(*read, not run:* the build confirms that command shows a fact's body, or adds the narrowest read
that does). These are ids, paths and commit ids. They pass the existing redaction and preview
steps, and no file content leaves the repo.

**R11.** `backlog.upstream.version_label` gains the plugin tree's short commit id when the running
version ends `-dev` and the plugin root is a git work tree. A released plugin keeps today's label.
The version is read from the manifest as now, never recalled. A version plus a commit is enough
to rule version skew in or out.

### D11 — Ledgers already in the bad state: no migration

Nothing rewrites a fact, and no doctor check rewrites state (1198-R6, data-model's append-only
norm). After upgrading:

- facts read exactly as before;
- every out-of-order `done` row shows D6's flag, worded for its route;
- every named `done` row shows D5's route, or that the route is not recorded;
- the remedy is one `plan retract <chunk> --all` per wrong chunk.

The proposal rule (D1) and the refusal (D3) act only at the next dispatch.

The backlog-roi plan in this repo is one such ledger. What it owes (crediting 02 to 05 and 07 to
10, and keeping or retracting 11) is a decision for the session that owns it, not part of this
build. While eight chunks are open, the archive sweep's `is_finished` precondition will decline it
at release.

## Risks

- **Adjacent path credit in whole-plan modes is not closed.** A `final` on 03 whose diff touches a
  path only 04 lists, with 04 next and open, still names 04: there is no gap, and D3's refusal is
  `chunk`-only. Ruling 2 settles R2 at (b), so this design does not reach for (c). D5 makes it
  diagnosable from `plan status` alone, and D6 does not flag it, since it is in order.
- **A delegated chunk with unscoped subjects, reviewed before an earlier chunk lands, loses its
  default name.** Its review names nothing and the chunk stays open. The dispatch note names
  `--chunks`, and `planning.md` (D9) says to use the scoped subject. This is the one shape the
  accommodation does not carry automatically. It fails closed.
- **Wording-pinned tests.** D5 puts text on every named `done` row, and D8 adds a clause to a
  pinned message. Expect assertion churn in the briefing and PR-payload tests. It is renegotiated,
  not relaxed.
- **A squash-merged part one reopens its chunks.** This is existing behaviour (row M6), made
  visible by ruling 1. D9 states it; the design does not change reachability.
